# HostPod: Own Your Cloud

We are renting our digital lives. 

Every photograph, every document, every line of communication has been quietly funneled into proprietary black boxes. We have surrendered our digital sovereignty to massive corporations that extract our data, lock us into their ecosystems, and force us to pay monthly rent to access our own memories and work. 

Worse, they engineer planned obsolescence. They tell us that a three-year-old smartphone, a device with a multi-core ARM processor, gigabytes of RAM, and an integrated uninterruptible power supply, is trash. They brick perfectly good hardware with locked bootloaders and abandoned software updates, creating mountains of e-waste simply to sell us the next iteration. 

This is a failure of the tech industry. It is time to build an exit. 

---

### I. The Cloud is Just Someone Else's Computer
True privacy does not exist on a server you do not physically control. If you cannot touch the drive your data lives on, it is not your data. We reject the proprietary ecosystems that hold our digital lives hostage. We are taking the infrastructure back into our own homes.

### II. Open Over Everything
Black boxes are the enemy of sovereignty. The future of personal computing must be radically transparent. We build exclusively on open-source foundations, open runtimes, open standards, and open operating systems. We do not rely on hidden APIs, we do not tolerate forced telemetries, and we despise closed-source operating systems that dictate what we can and cannot do with our own hardware.

### III. Resurrection Over Consumption
We already own the hardware required to run our digital lives. Millions of "obsolete" smartphones are sitting in drawers. These are not dead devices; they are dormant micro-servers. By stripping away the corporate bloatware and replacing it with a pure, headless Linux environment, we are turning discarded consumer electronics into powerful, localized infrastructure. 

### IV. Decentralization Requires Accessibility
For self-hosting to win, it cannot remain a hobby exclusively for system administrators. The barrier to entry must be shattered. Deploying a personal cloud, a photo backup server, or a private Git repository must be as simple as tapping a screen. Complex container orchestration must be invisible to the user, running silently and reliably on mobile silicon.

### V. Physical Resilience
Data sovereignty requires hardware reality. We do not trust fragile internal flash memory with our permanent records. We decouple the compute from the storage. The phone provides the brain and the battery backup; external, solid-state drives hold the soul.

---

### The Mission

We are building the bridge between discarded silicon and total digital independence. We are giving people the tools to host their own lives, on their own hardware, under their own roof.

**No subscriptions. No walled gardens. No gatekeepers.** 

Just your hardware, your software, and your data.
